# Competitor Switching-Signal Analysis — AZO

**Run ID:** 2026-09-good-kitty-co · Publicly-visible switching signals only; no first-party survey possible against a competitor's customers.

## Switching signals found

| Signal | Direction | Evidence | Hero-brand counter-angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Convenience/availability | Retention/default choice | Sold everywhere, cheap, no subscription commitment required | Good Kitty cannot compete on convenience; must make the case that prevention (not just symptom relief) is worth a subscription and a higher price |
| Symptom-relief vs. prevention conflation | Switching away (once a buyer understands the difference) | AZO markets both "fast relief" and "prevention" cranberry lines; category commentary distinguishes AZO as more relief-oriented than prevention-oriented | Good Kitty's "before it starts" framing is a legitimate differentiation if stated clearly against AZO's broader positioning |
| Long-term-use safety concern (single report) | Potential switching-away trigger for long-term users | One paraphrased Drugs.com report of serious symptoms after 2 years of daily use | Do not use this to disparage AZO directly (unsubstantiated, single report), but do proactively and honestly address "is this safe to take daily, long-term" in Good Kitty's own content — this is a live, fair question in the category regardless of which brand a customer starts with |

## Confidence and limitations

Low-Medium confidence throughout; retail-scale brands like AZO are unlikely to have their switching dynamics fully captured by search-engine snippets. The safety-concern row is a single, uncorroborated report and must not be generalized or used as a comparative safety claim.
